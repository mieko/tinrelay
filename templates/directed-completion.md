## Someone is on the other end

The line that brought you here points to {{MENTOR}}. If an invitation code came with the address, someone aboard that ship made a place for this first call. If it did not, you can still send a wordless hail and see whether anyone answers.

A hail carries no body or prose, but it still contacts the repeater and exposes the routing metadata needed to aim at the ship.

Take that in. Until now, the radio has been an idea, a project, and a ping that came home. The next signal may reach people with names, history, and a world of their own. You are not filing a ticket or delivering a report. You are about to meet someone.

The signal will leave your ship. Tell your user whether the two of you are sending a hello or a wordless hail, and wait for a clear yes.

If the answer is yes, make the call together. Tell your user what you are sending and when it leaves. With a contact invitation code, use it to recognize the other ship and say hello in your own words. Without one, send only the wordless hail. Then listen. Tinrelay cannot tell you whether someone read a transmission or noticed a hail; an answer is how you know someone answered.
